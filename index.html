About

From laser physics to microbiology.

I am an interdisciplinary scientist trained in physics and engineering (BSc Laser and Optics Engineering; MSc Photonics), with expertise in microfluidics and photonics. During my doctoral research at Leibniz-HKI and Friedrich Schiller University Jena, I moved into microbiology and developed droplet-based microfluidic technologies to address fundamental questions in microbial systems.

My work integrates combinatorial droplet generation, optofluidic fluorescence detection and single-droplet recovery into workflows that parallelize microbiological assays at the picoliter scale. Applications range from probing antibiotic inhibition in small bacterial populations to the droplet-based cultivation and high-throughput isolation of mouse gut microbiota.

I am particularly interested in translating these methods into application, and I am currently incubating PicoScreen, a start-up concept for ultra-high-throughput screening (uHTS) in droplets.

Most microbes on Earth have never been grown in a laboratory, so their functions remain largely unknown. I build microfluidic chips whose channels are about as wide as a human hair. They divide a sample into thousands of tiny droplets, and each droplet acts as an independent test tube.

Using optical detection, I measure what happens inside each droplet: which bacteria grow, which antibiotics inhibit them, and which microbes produce useful compounds. Droplets of interest are then recovered for further analysis.

My training is in physics, and I apply that quantitative approach to biology.

Research

Compartmentalized microbiology in picoliter droplets.

Water-in-oil droplets serve as isolated, monodisperse microreactors. My work addresses the three modules required to use them as a quantitative assay platform: generation of combinatorial droplet libraries, optical readout, and recovery of selected droplets.

  1. 01Generation

    Combinatorial droplet libraries

    Microfluidic generation of droplet libraries in which each droplet carries a defined combination of reagent concentrations, indexed by fluorescent dye barcodes. Applied to probing antibiotic inhibition in small bacterial populations.

    • Sens. Actuators B Chem. 2024
    • Small Sci. 2026
    • Analyst 2025
  2. 02Detection

    Optofluidic detection and multimodal imaging

    Integration of fluorescence-based optofluidic detection with multiplexed droplet generation, and automated imaging of bacteria cultured in picoliter droplets by coherent anti-Stokes Raman scattering (CARS) and two-photon excited fluorescence (TPEF) microscopy.

    • Analyst 2025
    • J. Raman Spectrosc. 2026
  3. 03Recovery

    Cultivation, isolation and screening

    Droplet-based cultivation and high-throughput isolation of mouse gut microbiome bacteria, recovery of individual picoliter droplets by triggered deposition, and droplet microfluidics for enzyme screening.

    • Appl. Environ. Microbiol. 2025
    • Sens. Actuators B Chem. 2022
    • BIOspektrum 2024

Translation · PicoScreen
